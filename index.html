01 Research

Most of the galaxies I find exceed the ionizing efficiency required to drive cosmic reionization — which makes them the best local laboratories we have for an epoch we can barely resolve directly.

First author

Finding extreme emission-line galaxies in J-PAS

J-PAS observes the sky through 56 narrow optical filters, giving every galaxy something close to a low-resolution spectrum. I built a method that combines narrow-band equivalent widths with machine learning to pull extreme emission-line galaxies (EELGs) out of that data set — the first such identification in the survey.

  • 917 EELGs identified out to z = 0.8, selected above a rest-frame equivalent width of 300 Å.
  • 95% purity and 96% completeness down to iSDSS < 22.5 mag, validated against DESI DR1 spectroscopy.
  • A strong correlation between ionizing photon production efficiency ξion and EW([O III]), matching what is seen at both low and high redshift.
  • Most sources sit above the ξion threshold normally assumed for reionization.

First author

The mass–metallicity relation from z = 1 to 9 with JWST

How enriched a galaxy is with oxygen is best measured through the electron-temperature "direct method", but that requires the faint [O III] λ4363 auroral line — which only JWST can reach routinely at high redshift. I assembled a homogeneous sample from the public DAWN JWST Archive and traced the mass–metallicity relation across most of cosmic history.

  • 286 star-forming galaxies from z = 1 to z = 9, all with [O III] λ4363 detected in JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectroscopy.
  • An MZR slope of γ = 0.38 ± 0.09, spanning log(M★/M☉) = 6.77–10.5 and 12+log(O/H) = 6.9–8.4.
  • Stacking galaxies without individual λ4363 detections gives a similar slope but metallicities 0.2–0.3 dex higher at fixed stellar mass — a direct handle on auroral-line selection bias.
  • Relative N/O and He/H abundances indicate fast-track nitrogen and helium enrichment in the early Universe.

Giménez-Alcázar, A., Amorín, R., Vílchez, J. M. — accepted, Astronomy & Astrophysics (2026) arXiv:2605.05327

Second author

The light between galaxies, from 0.4 to 5 µm

Intracluster light is the diffuse glow of stars stripped from their host galaxies and left drifting in a cluster's potential. For the relaxed cluster RX J2129.7+0005 we combined 15 deep HST and JWST bands with MUSE integral-field spectroscopy to produce the most detailed spectrum of intracluster light measured in any single cluster so far.

  • 3696 intracluster-light maps spanning ~0.4–5 µm, built with the CICLE algorithm.
  • Stellar mass of log₁₀(M★/M☉) = 11.5–12.7 from parametric and non-parametric SED fitting.
  • Mean stellar age of 9.7–10.5 Gyr — an old, long-assembled population.

Jiménez-Teja, Y., Giménez-Alcázar, A., Dupke, R. A., et al. — The Astrophysical Journal 974, 309 (2024) arXiv:2408.11122

Third author

Green peas, blueberries and the k-MENDEL sample

Green peas and blueberries are compact, metal-poor, violently star-forming dwarfs — the closest thing the local Universe offers to a reionization-era galaxy. The k-MENDEL sample assembles them spectroscopically from DESI and characterises the conditions that let ionizing photons escape.

  • Spectral identification of EELGs in DESI, covering blueberries and green peas out to z ≈ 0.35, plus redder EELGs at higher redshift.
  • Targets the low-metallicity regime where feedback and Lyman-continuum escape can be studied directly.

Bonatto, L., Amorín, R., Giménez-Alcázar, A., et al. (2026) arXiv:2604.09516

Earlier work

Machine learning for a dark matter detector

Before moving to galaxies I worked at CIEMAT on DEAP-3600, a 3.3-tonne liquid-argon dark matter detector two kilometres underground at SNOLAB. My work there applied machine learning to reconstruct where inside the argon volume each scintillation event occurred — the step that defines the fiducial volume and rejects background.

  • Neural-network event position reconstruction benchmarked against two maximum-likelihood algorithms.
  • Contributed to the collaboration's precision 39Ar measurements and its profile-likelihood dark matter search.

DEAP Collaboration — JINST 20, P07012 (2025) arXiv:2503.10383

04 About

I am a PhD candidate at the Instituto de Astrofísica de Andalucía (IAA-CSIC) in Granada, where I study how the most extreme star-forming galaxies in the nearby Universe can stand in for the ones that ended the cosmic dark ages.

My background is split between two very different kinds of physics. I trained at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid and spent two years at CIEMAT applying machine learning to a liquid-argon dark matter detector, before moving into extragalactic astrophysics. That detour left me with a working habit I have kept: treat a survey as a data problem first, and let the statistics decide what counts as a detection.

Day to day that means narrow-band photometry from J-PAS, integral-field spectroscopy from MEGARA and MUSE, direct chemical abundances, and increasingly JWST data at high redshift.
